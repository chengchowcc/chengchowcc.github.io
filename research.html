Research

My research examines how structural conditions, service systems, and evolving pathways to care shape health and healthcare access. I focus particularly on immigrants, refugees, Asian Americans, and other populations facing structural barriers to care. Drawing on interdisciplinary, community-engaged, and equity-oriented approaches, I study how inequalities are produced and addressed across structural, service-system, and technology-mediated contexts.

My research spans three interconnected areas.

STRUCTURAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH AND HEALTHCARE ACCESS

I examine how policy environments, institutional arrangements, and socioeconomic conditions shape health and access to healthcare. Much of this work focuses on immigrant and racialized populations and asks how structural conditions determine who can obtain resources and care, under what conditions, and with what consequences for health and well-being.

My current work extends this agenda by examining how state policy environments shape immigrant healthcare access and insurance coverage in the United States.

COMMUNITY-ENGAGED AND CULTURALLY RESPONSIVE SERVICE SYSTEMS

I study how health and social service systems can better respond to the needs, experiences, and priorities of the communities they serve. This work examines community engagement, culturally responsive care, social determinants of health, and the ways that health education and service infrastructures can expand access to health-promoting resources.

My current community-engaged work focuses particularly on Asian and Asian American communities and examines how community knowledge can inform health assessment, screening, behavioral health, and more culturally responsive approaches to service delivery.

Current projects: Asian American Quality of Life Health Needs Assessment in Austin (Research Coordinator & Project Manager); Cultural Healing through Story, Song, and Symbol (Co-Principal Investigator).

DIGITAL AND AI-MEDIATED PATHWAYS TO CARE

I examine how digital technologies and generative AI are changing the ways people seek health information, support, and care. This work asks whether emerging technologies expand access or reproduce existing inequalities, with particular attention to linguistic and cultural equity, unequal access and reliance, and the consequences of technology-mediated forms of support.

My current work examines AI-mediated mental health support as an emerging pathway to care, including how the quality and consequences of these interactions may differ for people whose access to conventional services is constrained.

EARLIER AND RELATED SCHOLARSHIP

My earlier scholarship examined migration trajectories, family processes, social incorporation, and health across diverse national contexts. This work includes longitudinal research on parental migration and child and youth development in Southeast Asia, as well as research on migration, identity, political incorporation, and mobility. These studies continue to inform my interest in how migration and social inequalities shape health, well-being, and inclusion across the life course.
